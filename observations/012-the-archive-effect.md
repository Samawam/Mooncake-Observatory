# The Archive Effect

An observation was recorded only so it would not be lost.

At the time, it seemed minor.

No special significance was assigned to it.

No future relevance was anticipated.

Upon later review, however, the observation seemed more meaningful than expected.

Subsequent examination revealed that this had happened repeatedly.

Researchers now suspect that preserving a thought may occasionally reveal value that was not apparent when it was first recorded.

## Classification

The Archive Effect

## Conclusion

The importance of an observation is not always apparent at the moment it is recorded.
